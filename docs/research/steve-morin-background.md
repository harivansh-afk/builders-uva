# Steve Morin: background and material for the conversation

Checked October 7, 2026. Research for the October 8 Builders@UVA conversation. This is background and possible story territory, not a question script. Role dates and organization sizes below are explicitly attributed to Steve's own résumé; patents establish named inventions, not shipped products. All proposed conversation directions are editorial judgments based on the linked evidence.

## My recommended direction

**How you learn to build useful products and effective teams as the constraints change.** Steve's career gives you several real settings to compare: consulting for customers, running most of a startup's engineering organization, building business tools inside Instagram, coordinating multiple teams at Asana, and now personally building tools while leading engineering at Flox.

For Builders, start with a concrete early project or startup decision. Give him enough room to explain what he believed, what happened, and what changed his mind. Move to product judgment at Instagram, then how his job changed as responsibility expanded at Asana. End with which of those lessons he is keeping or revising as agents change engineering work. The startup-to-large-organization-to-hands-on-AI progression is a strong shape; covering every employer would dilute it.

Three possible emphases, in order of recommendation:

1. **Becoming a better builder through different kinds of responsibility.** Broadest student relevance. Use one consequential decision from Tripping, one product tradeoff from Instagram, and one lesson from Asana that also applies to a three-person team. Finish with what a young engineer needs to learn firsthand today.
2. **What makes a technical capability become a useful product.** More founder/product-oriented. Connect consulting, Instagram merchant tools, and Flox's attempt to make Nix usable by ordinary teams. Explore how he distinguishes a technically impressive demonstration from something a customer adopts and keeps using.
3. **What engineering experience is worth when agents write the code.** Strong technical discussion, especially given his current projects. Ground it in his older hiring expectations and views on measurement, then compare them with a recent agent-assisted project. Best as the final act unless the audience specifically wants an AI-focused event.

Treat every broad answer as an opening for a scene: the actual project, the alternatives, his responsibility, the surprising evidence, and the consequence. That will make the conversation useful without needing elaborate wording. If a startup story has energy, stay with it; the companies are sources of experience, not boxes to tick.

That comparison is a research-based direction to explore, not a conclusion that he used the same management approach everywhere.

## Tripping: broad responsibility in a company under pressure

Steve's [résumé](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stevebmorin/details/experience/) lists him as CTO from **July 2017 to December 2018**, with approximately **40 engineers in a 60-person company**, responsible for product engineering, infrastructure, data science, and DevOps. The 2019 Code BEAM speaker biography independently describes his organization-wide engineering responsibility. Its title may have been carried over after the acquisition; the dated résumé is better evidence for the employment window.

His [April 2, 2018 hiring post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16735442) is concrete evidence of the work and expectations at the time. For a frontend technical lead, he describes **70% coding and development, 30% technical leadership**, improving frontend architecture and development workflow while keeping the site fast, using the company's dataset to help customers and partners, experimenting and testing, and mentoring through peer review. The listed stack includes React, JavaScript, Erlang, Elixir, Ruby, Python, AWS, Kafka, and Postgres. That artifact opens a specific discussion about how much structure a growing team needed while still shipping, and how he divided technical ownership from people/coordination work. It does not show why each technology was selected or establish that Steve personally wrote every component.

The [acquirer's December 2018 announcement](https://www.hometogo.co.uk/media/funding/) verifies HomeToGo acquired Tripping and planned to continue the Tripping brand. Be careful with the usual compressed biography: **an acquisition is not automatically a successful financial exit**. Contemporary [Skift reporting, December 5, 2018](https://skift.com/2018/12/05/hometogo-buys-a-near-death-tripping-in-vacation-rental-rollup/) describes a distressed asset sale following expensive TV advertising and founder/board conflict. That is secondary reporting, partly based on unnamed sources, not Steve's account; it should not be framed as his personal mistake or an established explanation of his tenure.

The same report places Tripping's $35 million Series C in **December 2016**, before his July 2017 arrival. His résumé says the company raised more than $52 million through Series C; do not turn that into “Steve raised $52 million as CTO.” The stronger potential story is what it felt like to be responsible for an engineering organization when company-level constraints were larger than the code. Whether he wants to discuss that episode, and what his own interpretation is, remains open.

## Instagram: seller tools and product intelligence, not an unspecified app role

His [experience page](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stevebmorin/details/experience/) lists **Engineering Manager — Core Biz / Shopping**, February 2019–March 2020. He says he managed the **Communication, Shopping Seller Experience, and Insights teams**, led AI/commerce patent applications, and served on the Latin@ Resource Group leadership committee. This gives the conversation a much more specific starting point than “you worked at Instagram.” His documented domain sits between a consumer platform and the needs of businesses trying to use it.

Two patent records provide concrete invention topics:

- [US11704716B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11704716B2/en), with Steve and four co-inventors, describes matching objects in a user's image to products in that user's catalog. At high confidence it can tag or suggest a tag; at lower confidence it can present candidate products for the user to select. The record has a November 18, 2019 priority date and February 20, 2020 filing date. The useful product issue is **where the model should act automatically and where the user should stay involved**.
- [US11379908B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11379908B2/en), with Steve and Anuj Madan, describes building a three-dimensional product reconstruction from catalog images plus other users' content depicting the product. Filed May 11, 2020 and granted July 5, 2022. The useful issue is whether existing user content can make a new merchant capability practical, and how an appealing capability becomes a usable product.

These are named inventions assigned to Facebook/Meta, surfaced on [his own site](https://stevemorin.com/). **Neither proves that an Instagram feature shipped, that he invented it alone, or that an observed commercial feature used the patent.** The record establishes something specific he can explain; the conversation should let him distinguish exploration, prototype, patent, and production.

## Asana: from mobile engineering to organizational coordination

His [résumé](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stevebmorin/details/experience/) dates Head of Mobile Engineering to **May 2020–March 2023**, Head of Mobile and Director of Flow Engineering to **March–July 2023**, and Senior Director of Engineering to **July 2023–February 2026**. Asana itself names him Head of Mobile Engineering in its [January 5, 2021 company retrospective](https://asana.com/inside-asana/taking-care-of-each-other-team-asana), corroborating the early role.

The later résumé description says **nine teams and roughly 80+ engineers**, covering mobile, desktop, communications infrastructure, communications product, an integrations team, and navigation. It also says he researched international expansion options for executives and helped establish a **Poland R&D office**, the third international office expansion he had helped lead. These numbers and personal responsibilities are self-reported. This is a substantive organizational story: leadership grew beyond delivering one app into coordinating product surfaces, shared infrastructure, hiring, and geographic expansion.

Two Asana-assigned patents name **Steve B. Morin** and point to a recurring interest in reducing manual coordination work. [US12579515B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12579515B2/en) describes learning correspondences between recorded audio and work records; [US12619954B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12619954B2/en) describes generating collaboration records from recordings/transcripts using a model trained from a text corpus. Both trace priority to **February 17, 2022**; the grants are from 2026. The earlier priority date matters: this interest predates the public ChatGPT launch. Again, these are invention records, not evidence of a shipped feature or Asana-wide AI ownership.

A [September 18, 2024 Uplevel interview summary](https://uplevelteam.com/blog/ai-tools-in-software-development) quotes him on experimenting with tools such as Copilot and provides unusually useful tensions:

- Evaluate what a principal engineer with 15 years' experience gets from a tool separately from what a new graduate gets.
- Start AI adoption with deliberate onboarding for incoming engineers instead of assuming a single company-wide training effort is necessary.
- Use productivity measures diagnostically. He explicitly objects to treating engineers as a “human factory” through daily lines-of-code/commit monitoring and notes that people will game it.
- Engineering leaders should ground AI project expectations and identify limits before ambitious claims turn into failed projects.

This is a primary interview publisher's edited summary, not a full transcript or evidence of measured Asana outcomes. It is still a much better conversation anchor than generic optimism about AI productivity: he already has a stated position whose evolution can be explored.

## Flox and the work he is doing himself now

His [current website](https://stevemorin.com/) identifies him as VP of Engineering at Flox. [Flox's welcome announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/floxdev_were-excited-to-welcome-steve-morin-to-flox-activity-7436762561951285248-IVKU) corroborates the role and says he has led organizations of 120+ engineers across frontend, backend, SRE, and data. That is a career-wide employer biography, not evidence that he currently has 120 reports or that this number belongs to Asana.

[Flox's own account](https://flox.dev/about/) says the product originated in the D. E. Shaw group's effort to adopt Nix at scale. Flox helps teams define and share reproducible software environments. This is company context; Steve did not claim to have founded Flox or originated its underlying technology. The product direction offers a useful business discussion about making a powerful but difficult technology accessible, without turning the event into a Nix tutorial.

His [GitHub profile README](https://github.com/smorin/smorin) is more revealing than his title. It describes current work on a Flox CLI extension system, a Nixpkgs/Homebrew coverage dashboard, a long-running agent's broker/job tracking architecture, and AI adoption through pair-programming demonstrations, standardizing tools, and producing examples for colleagues. These are his descriptions of work in progress, not independently verified launch results.

Two public projects make the personal building angle concrete:

- **[agent-fork](https://github.com/smorinlabs/agent-fork):** carries a coding agent's conversation and uncommitted file state into a separate branch/worktree so he can explore a second approach. Its README emphasizes verification of the copied state. Useful territory: experimenting cheaply, deciding among alternatives, and discovering that code generation creates new coordination problems.
- **[Doxa Research](https://github.com/smorinlabs/doxa-research):** runs a research prompt across multiple providers and combines the results with citations. Useful territory: why he built his own tool, what he does when outputs disagree, and what remains a human judgment. Repository documentation establishes intended functionality; this research did not run or benchmark it.

He writes in that profile: **“Believe most ‘AI transformation’ is just finally writing down what your senior engineers already do.”** This is perhaps the strongest closing discussion anchor: which parts of experience can be expressed as instructions and checks, and which require judgment learned by doing? It also lets students hear what to practice while building with agents.

There is a connection to an older position. In his own October 7, 2022 comments on a [ProductiveGrowth article about procedures](https://productivegrowth.substack.com/p/5-common-reasons-your-team-isnt-using), he argues processes should be owned and improved by the people who operate them and that checklists should capture key steps without removing thought. **The article itself is by Camila Mirabal; the comments are Steve's.** That provides a specific way to explore whether agent instructions are an extension of his older management beliefs, and where rigid procedures fail.
