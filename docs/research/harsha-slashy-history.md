# Harsha Gaddipati: public founder history

Research checked September 13, 2026. Background for a Builders@UVA conversation; this is a research memo, not public event copy.

The useful interview thread is how he learned to recognize behavior that matters: people joining a campus experiment, actually meeting their matches, returning to a product, and trusting it with daily work. That connection is an editorial interpretation of the evidence below, not a story Harsha has explicitly told in those terms.

The public record supports several distinct projects and changes of direction. It does **not** support describing all of them as one company that changed from dating to email.

## Chronology and evidence

### During COVID: learning to build for himself

In a later interview, Harsha says he taught himself programming to make a cricket general-manager game he wanted to play. This is a personal account; the game's name and release date were not established. [Slash founder interview, August 24, 2026](https://www.slash.com/blog/the-founders-who-think-email-may-be-the-key-to-agi).

### February 2023: Georgia Tech Dating Blind / GT Match

An original campus interview identifies Harsha as a first-year CS student and GTDB president, working with Julie Young. The team tested its survey on 40 people before the wider launch; the newspaper reported over 1,000 responses by February 8. It also described recommendations for date locations and possible expansion into roommate/friend matching. These are contemporaneous reports, not later startup metrics. [Technique, February 10, 2023, page 8](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b661e0b4-0297-4e27-a621-4d5b4094a731/content).

Harsha's June Q&A fills in the origin. A friend showed him Northwestern's Marriage Pact; he investigated why Georgia Tech lacked a continuing equivalent. He and friends organized a campus matching event, starting with a Microsoft Form. He says they expected roughly 100 responses and received over 1,000. Earlier campus attempts had created privacy fears, so transparency about data handling became part of earning participation. He also describes learning users' needs through conversations rather than assuming he understood dating. His team grew from four to over 20, then shrank to six; that especially hurt distribution. He called the broader organization GT Match. [Harsha's first-person VoyageATL Q&A, June 1, 2023](https://voyageatl.com/interview/community-highlights-meet-harsha-gaddipati-of-gt-match/).

**Interview opportunity:** What did he need to build before asking students to try it? What could remain manual? Who persuaded the first strangers to participate?

### 2024: Bubble Date, a separate earlier venture

Georgia Tech's Grand Challenges program says Harsha became Bubble Date's tech lead in 2024, working on matching. The same profile describes his earlier student project on black ice and road salt: the team interviewed people on Reddit, then concluded the problem belonged more naturally in specialized academic research. That is an earlier example of choosing whether the team was suited to a problem. The program page is undated; its text describes him as approaching graduation. [Georgia Tech Grand Challenges profile](https://grandchallenges.gatech.edu/linkedin-stories).

The September campus interview identifies the Bubble Date team as Sugju/Sugi Choi, Michael Klikushin and Harsha, with Harsha as CTO. Choi's experience with group dating informed the product: groups of friends matched with other groups, reducing the pressure of one-to-one introductions. Harsha said the difficult part was getting matched people to meet, alongside stigma around dating apps. The article reports about 1,000 users in under a month, with 900 signups attributed to campus flyers; Harsha estimated roughly 40% met their matches. These are historical claims by the team, without definitions or independently reviewed analytics. [Technique, September 20, 2024, page 11](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/fafd04ec-1e59-424c-9b3e-b0ab80a084ab/content).

Sugi later says she found Harsha through a Reddit post about a matching algorithm and contacted him. Her retrospective names Harsha and Michael as former cofounders and reports 3,000 active users, an event with 1,400-plus attendees, and $15,000 MRR for two months. Treat these as her account; the short duration matters, and the post does not establish what produced the revenue or why the venture ended. [Sugi Choi's post, December 26, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sugju-choi_my-superpower-is-finding-the-top-1-talent-activity-7410374879268765696-4SPP).

**Interview opportunity:** Which result mattered more than registrations? What changed after observing how people behaved outside the app? How did he decide whether to keep working on the venture?

### Before May 2025: meeting the next team and the website-builder phase

The later Slash interview says Harsha met Dhruv Roongta and Pranjali Awasthi through Georgia Tech's Startup Exchange. They joined forces after their own earlier ventures. Their initial Dash product generated websites, then became a work agent. Harsha attributes the website-builder change to limited repeat use. Exact starting dates are not established. [Slash founder interview, August 24, 2026](https://www.slash.com/blog/the-founders-who-think-email-may-be-the-key-to-agi).

**Interview opportunity:** What did the founders want from working together before they agreed on a product? Which customer behavior made a new direction seem warranted?

### May 4, 2025: graduation and a public Dash launch

Harsha's graduation post says he completed a Georgia Tech bachelor's in CS with highest honors after three years. He describes work at Amazon and State Farm, first-author research, teaching-assistant work and campus-supported startups. He also links the Dash Product Hunt launch. This is direct evidence that **Harsha graduated**; the common dropout narrative does not fit him. [Harsha's graduation post, May 4, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/harsha-gaddipati-032bb820a_georgiatech-graduation-activity-7324763127533707265-DJBp).

Product Hunt records Dash as its number-one product for May 4. In maker comments, Pranjali identifies Harsha and Dhruv as her cofounders. The launch pitches an agent acting across connected work apps. Harsha contrasts cross-app work with browser research; a customer asks whether emails retain their tone, and Pranjali says email is the first place they are applying memory. This matters: email was already an important workflow before the later email-client pivot. The page has editable current copy, so maker comments and the dated award are stronger historical evidence than its present headline. [Dash's original Product Hunt launch](https://www.producthunt.com/products/dash-11?launch=dash-1319).

### September 2025: traction and learning before the email pivot

In a video interview published September 2, Harsha describes approximately 10,000 lifetime users and 1,000 daily users for the agent. He separates acquiring attention from retaining people, emphasizes conversations with customers whose working lives he does not yet understand, and suggests that returning despite a rough product is evidence of real need. The recording date is unknown, and the automatic transcript has obvious transcription errors; use the clip to understand his thinking, not to present precise current metrics. [Viraj Ala's original interview with Harsha, September 2, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/viraj-ala-30390a154_i-ran-into-harsha-gaddipati-the-founder-activity-7368714349345009664-im5q).

On September 4, Harsha's HN launch explicitly calls Slashy a general agent, introduces Pranjali, Dhruv and Harsha, and says the preceding product was a code-generation agent similar to Lovable. He describes administrative work consuming the team's time and noticing four hours a day in Gmail. The product then connected across 15 services; email was one among multiple workflows. The post's architecture, integration count and free credits describe that version. [Harsha's original HN launch, September 4, 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45129031).

**Interview opportunity:** What evidence could distinguish strong early curiosity from a product worth committing years to? Ask for one actual user conversation, not a general rule.

### October–November 2025: moving into the email client

In the Alloyed interview, Harsha explains that people used the general agent after other assistants failed, making its opportunity vulnerable as those assistants improved. Email offered a place where work begins. The interviewer dates the pivot to October 2025 and self-serve launch to May 2026. Those dates are retrospective; they are not contemporaneous release records. [Alloyed's original interview, June 24, 2026](https://goalloyed.com/hiring-founder/harsha-gaddipati-slashy/).

A November 21 post provides contemporaneous confirmation of the new direction: Harsha shares a landing-page critique by Ryo Lu and Aaron Epstein and invites people to an email-product waitlist. He also acknowledges substantial unfinished work and the growing importance of product details. [Harsha's email waitlist post, November 21, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/harsha-gaddipati-032bb820a_really-appreciated-ryo-lu-and-aaron-epstein-activity-7397650041139654661-nqkm).

**Interview opportunity:** Which particular customer made email feel like the right starting point? What did the first usable version have to do reliably? What was deliberately left out?

## What the record does not settle

- **One legal company throughout:** No evidence establishes GT Match, Bubble Date and Dash as the same entity. Describe an earlier venture, then a new team/product.
- **Exactly why Bubble Date ended or Harsha left:** Not established. Ask openly; do not supply a failure explanation.
- **Exact website-builder dates or cohort metrics:** Not established. The two later interviews attach a 10,000-user figure to different product phases. Do not combine their numbers into one verified cohort. [Slash](https://www.slash.com/blog/the-founders-who-think-email-may-be-the-key-to-agi), [Alloyed](https://goalloyed.com/hiring-founder/harsha-gaddipati-slashy/).
- **A precise public pivot day:** The dated August rename and September general-agent launch precede November's email waitlist. Ask Harsha when the decision was made rather than repeating a collapsed retrospective chronology.
- **A forced move out of a non-AI niche:** The immediate predecessor to email was already AI. It is fair to discuss successive choices, but not to invent a crisis or imply that adding AI alone was the pivot.
- **Verified product-market fit, durable revenue or current retention:** None of the public sources reviewed supplies independent cohort data. Launch rankings and cumulative users do not establish these.

## Talking-point opportunities, in order of usefulness

These are suggested lines of inquiry, not additional factual claims.

1. **Start with an audience he could reach.** Reconstruct the first campus launch: the first version, the first ten participants, and what made strangers trust it. Bring this back to things a UVA student can do this week.
2. **Find the behavior behind the signup.** Ask what users actually did after trying each product, and which behavior he initially overlooked. Let him explain the difference between a distribution problem and a weak reason to return.
3. **Reconstruct one decision to change direction.** Who first proposed it? What evidence did they show? What was the strongest argument for staying? What did they stop building the next day?
4. **Make the niche concrete.** Who was the first customer they could serve unusually well? Which task happened often enough to matter? Why was it worth replacing a familiar tool?
5. **Make the advice operational.** Give him a hypothetical student with a working demo and enthusiastic friends. Ask what he would test next and what result would make him change his mind.

Useful backups: recruiting a cofounder through something publicly built; customer interviews when he lacked professional domain experience; the different reliability expectations of an experiment and a daily tool. Avoid spending much of a 20-minute conversation on YC travel anecdotes, generic fundraising advice, or abstract AGI predictions.
