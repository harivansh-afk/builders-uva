# Constitution and CIO Agreement review

Reviewed: September 29, 2026. Scope: the final four-page **Builders at UVA — Constitution and Bylaws, 2026–2027**, its Markdown source, and the locally supplied signed **2026–2027 Agreement for a Contracted Independent Organization**. This report reviews policy and document consistency. It does not certify officer eligibility, member attendance, actual votes, legal status, or completion of training. No application fields or existing documents were changed during this review.

## Result

The constitution covers all eleven subject areas in UVA's published sample. It includes the current 2026–2027 nondiscrimination statement, three separate student offices, election and removal rules, finance controls, bylaws, optional advisors, and amendment rules. I found no basis to require SAF participation, a current bank account when no funds are held or requested, a faculty advisor, a fourth officer, required dues, or a completed future-trip budget solely for this application.

Before final submission, verify the actual initial adoption and officer authorization; decide whether to remove the nonstandard `builders@uva` alias; and address the full non-affiliation notice in published communications. Preserve the newer nondiscrimination wording instead of copying the older sample's clause. The [application review](cio-policy-review-2026-09-29.md) covers actual active membership and event participation evidence, the live site, and the final declaration.

## Findings that need attention

### 1. Initial adoption and officer authority need factual confirmation

**Evidence:** C says September 29, 2026 is the adoption date (p.1). It calls its officers elected, provides regular March elections and May–April terms, and sets a 14-day roster period for voting eligibility (Articles III–V, pp.1–2). A §1, p.1 states that the signatory is the elected President or their designee, has member authorization to execute the agreement, and acts for an association operating under its constitution. A §1 also requires the club to follow its own amendment process.

**Assessment:** An adoption date is not evidence that members adopted the document or authorized the initial officers. The inspected documents do not show that evidence. This is a factual confirmation, not a finding that the date is false. Initial ratification can establish the governing rules; a rule in the new document should not automatically be applied backward to invalidate that initial act. However, after adoption the voting and notice requirements must be applied consistently.

**Before submission:** Confirm that September 29 is the real adoption date and retain a short record of the actual initial approval, initial officers, and President's authority. Do not invent a vote count, quorum, earlier notice, or election. Clarify whether roster join dates mean actual club membership dates or the date someone was entered into Hoos Involved; a recent bulk import should not silently erase genuine membership history. If no initial selection or authorization occurred, obtain it through a valid agreed founding process before relying on the signed representation. A short founding transition clause is an option only if members actually agree to it; it is not a substitute for approval.

### 2. The alias creates a naming review risk

**Evidence:** C Article I calls the organization Builders at UVA, also known as `builders@uva` (p.1). A §7, p.4 restricts use of the University's name and marks, including names and domains. T Article I, p.1 requires the `at UVA` form for a name that uses UVA. R explicitly accepts that form.

**Assessment:** **Builders at UVA** fits the Student Council instruction. The alias substitutes a symbol for the required phrase. I did not find primary guidance explicitly approving `@uva` as an equivalent. This is a likely reviewer question, not a proven determination that the alias is prohibited.

**Smallest resolution:** Use Builders at UVA consistently in the formal constitution and application. Separately check the website/domain and social names against the name-and-mark rules; removing an alias from one PDF does not resolve those surfaces. Do not treat an informal brand's prior use as University permission.

### 3. The short independence statement does not replace the prescribed notice

**Evidence:** C Article II says the club is independent and student-led but does not contain the full prescribed non-affiliation statement (p.1). A §6, pp.3–4 requires the specified statement in communications, publications, websites, social pages, and third-party dealings. P's Terms and Conditions, Dealings with Third Parties, also covers written communications and all website pages.

**Assessment:** The sample does not identify the full notice as a separate mandatory constitutional article. Still, the signed agreement's broad publication and communication rule matters when the constitution is published or sent to others. The brief independence sentence is not the prescribed statement.

**Resolution:** Add the full official notice to the published constitution or its publication context as appropriate, and make the website/social changes established by the separate live-site review. Use the statement from A §6, p.4 or the current UVA guidance verbatim; do not substitute this report's paraphrase. The required live application acknowledgement must reflect actual compliance.

### 4. No-funding status and future finance controls are compatible, with one clarification

**Evidence:** C Article VII declines SAF/Cav Funds for 2026–2027 and describes only possible future fundraising/donations and travel costs (p.3). It also says club funds are kept in a separate organization account. A §8, p.4 permits an Alumni Association account or independent setup; A §12, pp.7–8 requires an external organization account and supplier setup when University funds are allocated directly to the CIO.

**Assessment:** Not receiving funds and not having an account does not itself breach those conditions. The constitution's account sentence is a future control if the club later holds money, not proof that an account exists now. No-SAF and no-Cav does not cover every possible University funding source: §12 lists other routes too.

**Suggested clarification:** State in the application that the club holds no funds and has no account now. If desired, make the constitutional sentence explicitly conditional on receiving or holding funds. Do not promise to process donations through personal accounts or represent a possible grant as already received. The signed agreement says Alumni Hall accounts are automatically registered as suppliers; separate supplier steps apply to other account routes (A §12, p.7).

### 5. Follow-through duties remain after the President signs

These duties are not all required to be copied into the constitution, but must be carried out under A:

- **At least two executive-board members** participate each academic year in the education programs specified by Student Affairs, including the four topics listed in A §3, p.2. The agreement also calls for CIO Summit and Roundtable participation. The President's transition training alone does not establish that every annual duty is complete.
- Distribute the annual University hazing notice to the covered current and prospective participants (A §4, p.3). An open club without a pledge period still has this distribution duty. The additional extensive in-person new-member education requirement is tied to a membership invitation/initiation period; C does not establish such a period.
- Issue the liability and insurance notice to the covered participants (A §9, p.5). The University does not insure the club's activities.
- Keep names and roles current in Hoos Involved, with changes reflected within **48 hours** (A §1, p.1; §15, p.9). C assigns roster duties but does not repeat this deadline.
- Monitor the shared CIO email and route official notices to the responsible officers (A §14, pp.8–9; concluding responsibilities, p.10). `team@uva.builders` is a shared club address rather than a student's personal mailbox, consistent with the stated purpose.
- Use event-specific approvals and insurance when applicable (A §13, p.8). The insurance trigger can be a risk designated by staff even without minors. Do not treat “no minors” as a blanket insurance exemption. Ordinary future-event planning is separate from evidence that the initial application is complete.

**Minor drafting ambiguity:** Article VIII permits majority changes to bylaws, while Article XI requires two-thirds for constitutional amendments. Because both are in one file, identify whether an edit changes a subordinate bylaw or constitutional authority before voting. This is an internal clarity issue, not a documented UVA rejection ground. Also apply consistent denominators when excluding an affected member from a removal vote; do not use exclusions to manipulate quorum.

## Required rules versus examples and older guidance

1. **Current nondiscrimination text:** Keep C's current clause. A §10, pp.5–6 and U's 2026 constitution step use the HRM-009 reference. The February 2025 template and R display older enumerated lists. A calls acceptance of the current statement mandatory and its use in the constitution strongly recommended; U gives a direct replacement instruction. The app's current declaration is also relevant. Treat the older list as stale, not as a reason to remove the current clause.
2. **Officer eligibility:** A §1, p.1 allows part-time or full-time fee-paying students enrolled in a degree-granting program. O says full-time only; R adds on-Grounds enrollment. C's current-rule reference avoids asserting that the looser phrase “enrolled student” alone is enough. If any officer is part-time or remote, request clarification from Student Engagement before certifying eligibility. Do not silently resolve conflicting public summaries against the signed current agreement.
3. **Officer number:** U requires President, Vice-President, and Treasurer. R's new-CIO page states a minimum President and financial officer. C has all three, so this discrepancy does not cause a present gap.
4. **Examples are not universal rules:** T's Harry Potter activities, semester wait for office, sample quorum, and Secretary role are examples. The actual required subject is a clear workable process. The current constitution may use its own rules. Community membership and an advisor are recommended in T pp.6–7; neither is mandatory for this ordinary CIO. Parliamentary rules are a recommendation, not a required Robert's Rules adoption.
5. **Adoption is not recognition:** T's sample heading asks for recognition date/year. The club is still applying. C correctly labels its date as adoption, not University approval; no recognition date should be invented. A §17, p.9 says the agreement becomes effective only when Student Affairs approves it.
