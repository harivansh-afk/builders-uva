# Harsha Gaddipati — host notes

Prepared September 13, 2026. Suggested theme: **Building something people come back to.**

Event page: `/events/harsha-gaddipati`. Scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026. Time and location are to be announced.

The full public-history research and its limits are in [the research memo](../research/harsha-slashy-history.md). These are talking points for a 20-minute interview, not a list to get through. Ask one opening prompt per section, listen, and use the follow-up only if it gets to something his answer left out. Timings include the introduction; audience questions would be additional time.

## 0–3 minutes: Getting the first real users

Introduce him briefly: “Harsha is the co-founder and CEO of Slashy, an AI email company from YC's Summer 2025 batch. Before that, he was building dating products at Georgia Tech. I want to talk about how he decided what to keep building and what to move on from.” [Background: YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/slashy), [his early founder interview](https://voyageatl.com/interview/community-highlights-meet-harsha-gaddipati-of-gt-match/).

**Start with:** “Take us back to your first dating project at Georgia Tech. How did you get students to actually try it?”

Stay with the first thing he put in front of people, how he reached them, and what surprised him. This gives students a starting point they can picture.

If useful: “What did people actually do that was different from what they told you they wanted?”

## 3–7 minutes: Trying a product versus needing it

**Transition:** “Then came the website builder. What made you decide to move on from it?”

Let him explain the sequence. His account describes website-building demand that was largely occasional. The interesting part is how he recognized that, and whether he tried to solve it before changing products. [Background: Harsha and Dhruv's Slash interview](https://www.slash.com/blog/the-founders-who-think-email-may-be-the-key-to-agi).

If useful: “What were you watching then that made things look good—and what would you watch now?”

Avoid supplying a user count. Published interviews attach a similar headline count to different product stages; the precise cohorts are unclear.

## 7–12 minutes: The decision to pivot

**Start with:** “With the general AI assistant, you had people who liked it. What told you that wasn't enough?”

His explanation is that customers used it when larger AI assistants failed; improvements in those assistants threatened that use case. Explore the decision as it looked at the time, before the outcome was known. [Background: Harsha's Alloyed interview](https://goalloyed.com/hiring-founder/harsha-gaddipati-slashy/).

If useful: “Take me into the conversation where you decided to change direction. What was the strongest argument for sticking with it?”

Give this section the most room. A specific conversation, user observation, or experiment is more useful than a general rule about pivoting. Don't assume all the founders agreed immediately, or that they disagreed; let him describe it.

## 12–16 minutes: Choosing email and a first customer

**Start with:** “Email is a huge, crowded market. Who was the first specific person you wanted to make it better for?”

Keep the discussion on one user, one recurring task, and how he got that person to try a new product. Then ask what they came back to do. This tests whether the niche was a particular customer or job, rather than just the category ‘AI email.’

Email already featured in the earlier agent. Ask why it became the center of the product, not when he first discovered it. [Background: May 2025 Product Hunt maker comments](https://www.producthunt.com/products/dash-11?launch=dash-1319).

If useful: “What did you have to get right before that person trusted you with their real inbox?”

If he stays broad, ask for one customer's day before and after. Slashy's current customer stories cover both an investor's correspondence and a student creator's interview scheduling; use those as prompts if needed, not as independently measured results. [Investor story](https://www.slashy.com/case-study/priscilla-russo), [creator story](https://www.slashy.com/case-study/viraj-ala).

## 16–20 minutes: Bring it back to this room

**Close with:** “If you were back on campus with two weeks to test an idea, what would you do before writing much code?”

Try to leave the room with something concrete: whom to approach, what to ask them to try, and what behavior would earn another two weeks of work.

If useful: “What would make you stop?”

Don't force him into a universal prescription. Ask which parts of his experience transfer to a student starting today and which depended on his particular team or access.

## Keeping it conversational

- If an answer gets abstract: “Can you give me one example?”
- If he makes a strong claim: “What changed your mind?”
- If time gets tight, shorten the opening history and preserve the pivot decision and campus close.
- If audience questions must fit inside 20 minutes, use 0–2 for origins, 2–5 for repeat use, 5–10 for the pivot, 10–13 for email, 13–15 for the campus close, and 15–20 for the audience.

## Background cautions

The history spans several projects; don't present the dating app as an established company that directly became Slashy. Don't describe Harsha as a dropout. Avoid fixing an exact month for the email pivot: published accounts differ. These distinctions and the supporting sources are documented in the research memo.
