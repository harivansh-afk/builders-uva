# Alex Oliver: prep

Event: November 19, 2026, 6 PM ET, in person, capacity 100. Checked November 3, 2026.

## Who he is

- Partner, a16z American Dynamism, on the **Go-To-Market Network team**, not the investment team. He connects portfolio companies with the DoD; he is not the person who writes checks. The team page lists him first under "Operations Team", labeled Go-to-Market; the investment team lists the GPs plus investing partners such as Keil, Penny and McEntush. No found deal he led and no board seat.
- 15 years in the Army: infantry 2010–2013, then military intelligence. Deployed to Iraq, Afghanistan and West Africa; served as Director of Intelligence for a Joint Task Force. Last active-duty job: technology adviser to SOCOM leadership. Bronze Star.
- Began in Countering Weapons of Mass Destruction policy at the Pentagon. Then ran DIU's maritime autonomy portfolio: as MAJ Oliver he managed the A2RV amphibious mine-clearing drone prototype (Nauticus Robotics, 2022) and moved prototypes to the Navy and Marine Corps.
- 2021 Shift Defense Ventures Program fellow, placed at Lightspeed.
- Per the NSPC (an older page), still an Army Strategic Intelligence Officer in the Military Intelligence Readiness Command. MIRC is a U.S. Army Reserve command at Fort Belvoir, VA, whose strategic intelligence groups support the DIA. That makes him a part-time Army Reserve intelligence officer. The last rank found was Major (2022); his current rank is unknown.
- VMI (BA), American University (MA), National Intelligence University (MS). CFR member. Lives in Virginia.

## What he has said publicly (2026)

- **"No Man Left Behind"** (May 11), co-written with Daniel Penny and Zachary Chen. Argues that refusing to build autonomous weapons breaks the state's promise to its soldiers. Says Hiroshima saved lives. Barely addresses human control over targeting.
- **"How to Win a Space War"** (June 22, with Christian Keil). A 12,000-word report arguing that orbit is now a place where wars are fought; Space Force leadership got it before publication.
- **"Magazine Depth Affordability Is a Choice"** (Sept 15). Operation Epic Fury drained missile stocks. The FY27 munitions plan splits 97/3 between legacy weapons and cheap ones, and he calls that a "generational mistake." He promotes a16z portfolio companies Anduril, Castelion and Covenant and criticizes Lockheed and Raytheon.
- **"DoW Contracting for Startups 101"** (with McEntush and Hay).
- No 2026 podcast or panel appearances found, so this event may be one of his first public Q&As.

## Talk flow

1. Opener
   - Infantry, then intel, then DIU, then a16z. What was the moment you decided the answer was outside the Army?
   - What did six weeks inside Lightspeed show you that 12 years in uniform hadn't?
2. DIU
   - The mine-clearing drones: 67 bids, 2 picked. What actually separated the winners?
   - You got funding to back two vendors instead of one. Why does competition matter that much to the government?
   - Most DIU prototypes never become real programs. What kills them?
3. Selling to the government
   - What's the most common way a startup wastes its first year with the Pentagon?
   - Who actually has the money, and who do founders waste time on?
   - Taking government contracts versus raising VC money: when does each one trap you?
4. a16z
   - What does go-to-market at a16z actually look like week to week?
   - What does a portfolio company need from you that it can't get from an ex-general on its board?
5. Where it's going
   - You argue cheap missiles in volume beat expensive ones in small numbers. Who loses if you're right?
   - Space: what's the one thing a student could build there that the market is missing?
   - If you were a UVA junior today, what would you build?
6. Closer
   - Military or tech first: which is the better path for a student?
